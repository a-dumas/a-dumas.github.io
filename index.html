About

I am a fourth-year Computer Science PhD student at the University of Rhode Island and a member of the ASSET Lab led by Krishna Venkatasubramanian. My research focuses on smartphone-based digital phenotyping — the use of passive smartphone and wearable sensor data to model health-related states and behaviors.

I work on applied machine learning projects aimed at improving health outcomes for marginalized populations. My work leverages accelerometer, GPS, communication logs, wearable physiological signals, and other multimodal sensing streams to understand patterns, routines, and behavioral changes.

What I'm doing

  • Smartphone-Based Digital Phenotyping

    Developing scalable mobile sensing systems that transform passive smartphone data into clinically meaningful behavioral insights.

  • Applied Machine Learning for Health

    Designing predictive models to anticipate non-adherence and support just-in-time adaptive interventions.

  • Wearable Biosensing Systems

    Leveraging wearable physiological sensors to detect treatment non-adherence and substance use risk patterns.

Projects

  • SmartSteps: Improving PrEP Adherence using Digital Phenotyping

    Developing SmartSteps, a context-aware digital phenotyping model to anticipate PrEP nonadherence and deliver corrective feedback before nonadherence events occur. This project is led by Dr. Peter Chai (Brigham and Women’s Hospital & Harvard Medical School) in collaboration with Brigham and Women’s Hospital and Fenway Health.

    Funded by: NIH subaward via Brigham and Women’s Hospital

  • Wearable Biosensor-Based Opioid Abuse Treatment Management

    Developing wearable physiological sensing methods to detect medication non-adherence in medication-assisted treatment for opioid use disorder using wearable biosensors.

    Funded by: NIH NIBIB R01 Grant
